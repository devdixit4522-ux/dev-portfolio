The Problem

For a first-timer, Start a SIP is a list, standing in for a decision. I believed the freeze was choice overload. Nobody froze for lack of options. They froze waiting for a person to say it was okay.

Found

How might we

Give a first-time investor the go-ahead a brother would give — where to start and how much — without naming a fund.

What Each Side Needed

People

A normal place to start

To know what a normal, sensible starting point looks like, and feel confident it isn't a mistake.

A number from someone trusted

Someone they trust to give a specific number, so they don't have to work it out from scratch.

Not locked in

To know they can pause or stop if they need the money, so they don't feel financially trapped.

The Solution

Two screens. A guide on Start a SIP, then an investment plan, before the fund and the SIP amount.

Explore Start a SIP · guide + list Investment plan Fund SIP amount Done

Screen 1: A sentence that fills itself in

Live prototype · loops

Name a goal, a year and a target. Never the monthly amount.Cost. Four forced answers, no skip. The guide is a commitment, not a suggestion.

Screen 2: A group, not a name

The number first, then a group of two funds. Never one pick.Cost. Not naming one fund hands the last pick back. That's the freeze I set out to fix, one screen later.

Rejected Designs

Reads like a form to complete

₹1.5 lakh had to become "1 lakh" or "5 lakh"

First wireframe: one card, three questions

Fast: three answers on one card, then a starting zone. But preset chips forced a rounded goal (₹1.5 lakh had to become "1 lakh" or "5 lakh"), and a card of options still reads like a form to complete, not a go-ahead from someone.

Two amounts before the first fund

And a paragraph

Hi-fi result: a start and a full pace

Gentle and honest: a ₹2,000 start, with the ₹4,000 full pace beside it. But two amounts and a paragraph came before the first fund, so the ₹2,000 felt like a teaser and the ₹4,000 like the real price.
