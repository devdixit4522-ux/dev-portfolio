BookMyShow · Product design case study

Improving Event Discovery for BookMyShow

BookMyShow is an app people in India use to book movie tickets. It also sells tickets to comedy shows, concerts and sports, which I’ll call Events. Most people never see them. I redesigned how the app helps people discover Events.

Role

Product designer

Goal

More people booking Events

Help movie browsers discover other things to do

Most BookMyShow users open the app, go to Movies, and scroll through films first. When nothing feels interesting enough to watch, the app has other things to offer: comedy shows, concerts and sports. But those options are easy to miss.

I started by improving this drop-off point, then worked back to the homepage.

Users leave when they can’t find a movie worth booking

Most people who open BookMyShow with no plan in mind browse Movies, find nothing and leave. They came to buy something, and the app sold them nothing.

The people I designed for have no plan in mind, and leave because Events are hard to discover inside the app.

Recover users after movie discovery fails

The goal wasn’t to route users away from Movies. It was to offer another bookable plan without making it feel like a detour.

Where should Events appear without interrupting movie browsing?

I compared three places on the movie page.

1

A pop-up when the user is about to leave.

  • Catches the exit moment
  • Interrupts people
  • Feels promotional
  • Adds friction at the worst moment
2

A row of nearby events inside the movie page.

  • Keeps users in context
  • Doesn’t interrupt
  • Shows real alternatives right away
  • Users have to scroll to reach it
3

A card that opens Events categories.

  • Very visible
  • Adds another decision
  • Sends users down a new path
  • Doesn’t show alternatives right away

I went ahead with Events inside the movie page. They keep users in context, so the next option feels like a continuation, not a detour.

How should users evaluate an unfamiliar event?

Tapping an event could open its full page, or a quick preview. I compared both.

Live prototype · loops

1

Tapping the card opens the full event detail page.

  • Fastest path for users already interested
  • Moves users away from the movie page immediately

Live prototype · loops

2

Tapping the card opens a compact preview, then lets users view details or book.

  • Keeps users on the same page
  • Enough information to judge if the event is relevant
  • Adds one step before booking

I went ahead with a preview before commit. Tapping an unfamiliar event doesn’t mean users are ready to leave the movie page.

The problem was being solved too late

The Events row recovered users after they had committed to Movies. So I decided to move upstream, to the homepage, where that choice is first made.

Users choose Movies before deciding what they want to do

Most people I interviewed went to Movies first, even when they hadn’t decided what to watch. Two kinds of people were being pushed the same way:

“I don’t know what I want tonight.”

This person wants ideas, not a specific category.

“I want to go out — maybe not a film.”

This person wants to go out, but may not want a film.

Redesigning the homepage to help users choose what to explore

The audit: Movies dominated attention while Events were easy to miss

Movies dominate attention

Featured carousels, recommended films and promotional banners fill most of the feed.

Events are technically available

Comedy, plays and music appear mainly as small chips at the top. Participants often overlooked them or continued into Movies.

Discovery is fragmented

Each category opens separately, so it’s hard to compare movies, comedy, concerts and sports in one place.

Three homepage directions I explored

Most people went to Movies first, so the homepage had to help them choose a kind of plan before a film. Banner ads and “Browse by cinema” had to stay on the homepage, so every direction had to make room for them.

Two equal entries + mixed discovery

Movies and Events get equal doors at the top, with a mixed carousel below. Neither category is the default, and the ad stack stays intact.

Direction 1 · Chosen

  • Neither category is the default
  • The ad stack stays intact
  • Undecided users browse films and events together
  • Adds one more content layer

Tabbed home

Tabs for For you, Movies and Events put Events level with Movies. But they move the Movies-first habit one level down, and split ad impressions across three feeds.

Direction 2 · Rejected

  • Events sits level with Movies
  • Moves the Movies-first habit one level down
  • Splits ad impressions across three feeds
  • Browse by cinema only fits the Movies tab

Plan your evening

One mixed list of movies and events, sorted by start time, with date chips on top. It’s the closest to “What should I do tonight?”, but it leaves no room for banner ads.

Direction 3 · Rejected

  • Closest to “What should I do tonight?”
  • No room for banner ads
  • Needs a date filter to track
  • Users with a film in mind scroll a mixed list

What changed, and what still needs validation

What the research changed

The problem wasn’t that Events were hard to find.

Events weren’t shown while users were still deciding

Most people entered Movies first, even when undecided

Recovery on the movie page was necessary, but not enough

What the prototype showed

Events and Movies were both easy to find.

Open-ended browsing needs more users

No live traffic, so no real business metrics

Whether noticing Events turns into bookings is still unproven

The goal wasn’t to make users choose Events instead of Movies. It was to make sure that browsing Movies didn’t hide every other plan BookMyShow already sells.
