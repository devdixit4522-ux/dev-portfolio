Case Study

Improving Event Discovery

Overview

Users often entered Movies first and left when they couldn't find a film worth booking. I redesigned the discovery experience to surface Events at two points — before users choose a category and when movie discovery stalls.

Most BookMyShow users open the app, go to Movies, and scroll through films first. When nothing feels interesting enough to watch, the app has other things to offer — comedy shows, concerts, and sports — but those options are easy to miss. I started by improving this drop-off point, then worked back to the homepage.

Core Insight

A user may still want to go out even if they don't choose a movie.

The opportunity was not to push users into events. It was to show relevant events when users were still looking for something to do.

Recovering from a stalled search

The question wasn't how to route users away from Movies. It was where to place a bridge from a failed movie discovery without making the alternative feel like a detour.

The Reframe

But I was solving the problem too late. Why were undecided users entering Movies in the first place?

The homepage was pushing users toward Movies before they had chosen what kind of plan they wanted. If BookMyShow could surface Events before users committed to a category, we might prevent the dead-end entirely.

Before: Movies First

After: Equal Entry

Equal category entries

Give Movies and Events equal weight at the top of the homepage. Undecided users should be able to browse movies and events together in a mixed discovery carousel before choosing a category.

Mixed discovery feed

One carousel combining films, comedy, concerts, plays, and other bookable experiences. Lets undecided users browse across formats while preserving the existing ad inventory.

The Final System

Choose earlier.
Continue later.

The goal wasn't to make users choose Events instead of Movies. It was to make sure that browsing Movies did not hide every other plan BookMyShow already sells.
